# The Turko–Paradox War

The **Turko–Paradox War** began in September 2026 after a moderation dispute on the official *Hearts of Iron IV* Discord server escalated into a large-scale online confrontation between the Turkish gaming community and Paradox Interactive. What began as a single ban appeal developed within days into a coordinated protest involving Turkish streamers, historians, Steam users, Paradox communities abroad, Valve, and Paradox management itself.

The term **“Turko–Paradox War”** was publicly used during the conflict by the developer of *[Neolithic]To the End*. In the game's 25 September developer diary, a section was explicitly titled **“The Turko-Paradox war”**. The developer declared support for the Turkish side and added two portraits of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk to the game in response to the controversy.

---

## Origins of the conflict

The player at the center of the dispute, **Yiğit Emre Katran**, used the Discord account `sclunjstcwarrior`. According to Katran's later account, his Discord profile had first been compromised and used for spam, resulting in a ban from the official *Hearts of Iron IV* server. After recovering the account, he appealed the ban.

During the appeal, moderator **chakerathe** informed him that his real photograph of Mustafa Kemal Atatürk would have to be removed before the account could return to the server. The conflict therefore did not begin because the original spam ban had been issued over Atatürk; it began when the restoration of the account became conditional on removing Atatürk's photograph.

The dispute escalated because the moderation team did not simply cite a neutral rule against photographs of political figures. Atatürk was described as a **“controversial” and “polarizing”** figure relevant to modern politics, and the discussion went further by linking him to the **Armenian Genocide of 1915** and the Committee of Union and Progress. Turkish users understood the moderator's argument as effectively accusing Atatürk of involvement in the genocide and using that alleged involvement as part of the justification for treating him as a controversial figure.

This allegation became the central issue of the entire controversy.

Even scholarship that is highly critical of later Kemalist narratives distinguishes Atatürk from the perpetrators of the 1915 deportations and massacres. Historian Fatma Ülgen, for example, writes explicitly that **Atatürk took no part in the genocide of 1915**, noting that he was serving at Gallipoli and was not part of the CUP's central leadership responsible for those policies.

The Turkish reaction was therefore directed not merely at a profile-picture restriction, but at what participants regarded as a serious historical accusation made by a representative of an official Paradox community.

---

## 22–23 September: The Turkish community mobilizes

The case moved rapidly from Discord into Turkish gaming communities and social media. **CarrieRTV/Sefa** brought the dispute to his audience, while screenshots of the moderator conversations spread through X, Reddit and Steam discussions. According to participants interviewed by Turkish media, hundreds of users contacted Paradox support during the first organized wave of complaints.

As Turkish users entered the official server to protest the moderation decisions, the situation escalated rapidly. Messages criticizing the decision were deleted, users were removed from the server, and Paradox's official *Hearts of Iron IV* Discord **temporarily stopped accepting new members** as the backlash intensified.

The crackdown eventually resulted in **more than 300 Turkish users being banned** from the approximately 150,000-member Discord server. The growing number of bans became another major grievance for the Turkish side and transformed what had initially been a dispute involving a single account into a broader confrontation between the server's moderation team and the Turkish community.

**Maxmarine** and his strategy-game community became another major center of mobilization. The controversy spread between audiences that already heavily overlapped through *Hearts of Iron*, *Europa Universalis*, *Victoria* and other grand-strategy games.

Maxmarine and **MuratAbiGF** later publicly removed Paradox games from their computers during streams as a symbolic boycott action. **Yusuf Kayaalp**, who had previously worked with Paradox commercially, also warned that his relationship with the company could end unless the issue was addressed. **Jahrein** announced that he had personally contacted a senior Paradox executive regarding the dispute.

From the earliest public protest messages, three demands repeatedly emerged:

1. **Paradox should issue an explicit official apology.**
2. **The moderator responsible for the accusations and the escalation should be removed from his position.**
3. **Paradox should explicitly withdraw the accusation linking Atatürk to the Armenian Genocide and formally correct the historical record regarding Atatürk.**

One of the earliest Steam discussions stated directly that the moderator should be dismissed and that Paradox should apologize to the Turkish public for accusing the founder of the Turkish Republic of involvement in genocide. Similar demands appeared throughout the protest on X, Reddit, Steam and review platforms.

These demands would become important later because Paradox's eventual statements addressed the moderation rules, but did **not** satisfy all three.

---

## 23 September: Emrah Safa Gürkan joins the boycott

The dispute moved beyond gaming circles when historian **Prof. Dr. Emrah Safa Gürkan**, himself a long-time Paradox player with more than 6,000 hours in the company's games, publicly supported the boycott.

Gürkan described the incident as involving both hostility and historical distortion and stated that he would not continue playing Paradox titles unless the company corrected its position and apologized.

His intervention was important because the central accusation against Paradox was now being challenged not only by players and streamers, but also by a professional historian closely familiar with both Ottoman history and Paradox's games.

---

## The Steam offensive

The protest soon shifted to Steam.

Thousands of Turkish users posted negative reviews for *Hearts of Iron IV*, rapidly pushing its recent review rating into **“Overwhelmingly Negative.”** The campaign then spread to other Paradox titles, including *Europa Universalis*, *Stellaris*, *Crusader Kings* and *Victoria*.

By 25 September, one recorded snapshot showed **25,034 recent reviews with only 7% positive**, illustrating the extraordinary scale of the campaign.

The expansion beyond *Hearts of Iron IV* generated significant resistance in international Paradox communities. Many foreign users accepted that the original moderation decision was questionable but argued that review-bombing unrelated games was unjustified. Others openly supported the Turkish players and criticized the inconsistent enforcement of the Discord rules.

The conflict had therefore developed into two overlapping disputes: **Turkish players versus Paradox**, and **Turkish protesters versus sections of the wider international Paradox community**.

---

## The TommyKay controversy

One of the most visible confrontations with the foreign *Hearts of Iron IV* community involved German streamer **TommyKay**.

During his coverage of the controversy, TommyKay made statements generalizing and insulting Turkish users, including references to **“dumb Turks”** and broader comments about Turkish people and Turkish political culture. These comments were widely regarded by Turkish viewers as racist and anti-Turkish rather than as criticism limited to the review-bombing campaign. The clips were circulated extensively within Turkish gaming communities.

The dispute eventually resulted in a direct voice conversation between TommyKay and **Jahrein**, broadcast through Maxmarine's stream. Jahrein confronted him over his comments toward Turks and told him that, in his view, he deserved a **permanent ban** for what he had said.

TommyKay subsequently stated on his own stream that Twitch had suspended him for **seven days** over the incident. The ban became another major rallying point for the Turkish side of the dispute.

---

## 24 September: Paradox responds for the first time

On 24 September, *Hearts of Iron* Game Director **Batya** issued Paradox's first official response.

Paradox acknowledged Atatürk's significance as the founder of the Republic of Türkiye and stated that neither its games nor its moderation were intended to pass judgment on historical figures.

More importantly, the company explicitly conceded that the profile-picture rule **“has not been enforced consistently”** and announced that both the rule and the bans issued during the controversy would be reviewed.

This was the first major concession by Paradox: one of the central Turkish arguments, that the rule had been applied selectively, was now acknowledged by the company itself.

However, the statement did not directly apologize for the moderator's accusation against Atatürk, did not retract the Armenian Genocide allegation, and did not announce disciplinary action against the moderator. Turkish users therefore continued the campaign. Turkish gaming media explicitly noted that no direct apology had been issued.

---

## 25 September: Paradox accepts responsibility, but does not apologize

On 25 September, Paradox issued a second and significantly stronger statement.

Batya acknowledged that the company's handling of the situation had created the impression that Paradox itself had taken a position on Atatürk and accepted responsibility for what had occurred on the company's official Discord server.

The statement contained its strongest admission:

> **“This should have been prevented, and we failed.”**


Paradox also directly acknowledged the role played by the community campaign. Batya thanked those who had opened support tickets or sent messages to raise their concerns and stated that Paradox had **used those messages to help make the resulting changes**. In effect, the company publicly acknowledged that the organized wave of messages and support requests generated during the Turkish campaign had contributed to its review of the rules.

Paradox then completely rewrote the disputed profile-picture rule. Under the new policy, real photographs of political and military figures born after **1 January 1800** would no longer be permitted. Official *Hearts of Iron IV* portraits could still be used, with a separate restriction covering leading figures of Nazi Germany. Users were given a seven-day transition period.

The rule change addressed the inconsistent moderation system that had triggered the dispute.

**It did not, however, amount to the apology the Turkish protest movement had demanded.**

Paradox did not explicitly say **“we apologize”** for the treatment of Atatürk. It did not directly withdraw the moderator's claim connecting Atatürk to the Armenian Genocide. It did not publicly correct the historical accusation made against Atatürk. And it did not announce that chakerathe had been dismissed or disciplined. Turkish gaming coverage therefore summarized the situation on 25 September simply as **“there is still no apology.”**

This distinction explains why the second statement, despite accepting institutional responsibility and changing the rules, did not fully end the controversy.

From the Turkish side's perspective, the central dispute had never been only about whether photographs of historical figures were allowed on Discord. The dispute had become centered on an accusation that **Mustafa Kemal Atatürk was implicated in the Armenian Genocide**.

The Turkish campaign's demands were therefore not satisfied by replacing the profile-picture rule. Protesters were seeking a direct retraction of that historical accusation, an explicit apology, and accountability for the moderator who had made it.

---

## 25 September: A Paradox-owned studio is hacked

The conflict expanded beyond Discord, Steam and social media on 25 September when the support infrastructure of **Playrion**, a mobile-game studio owned by Paradox Interactive, was compromised.

Players of *Airlines Manager* reported receiving an email directly from the studio's official **`support@playrion.com`** address with the subject line:

> **“HACKED BY TURKS”**

The message identified the intrusion with the ongoing Paradox–Atatürk conflict and claimed that the attackers had obtained information connected to **more than 2.2 million user accounts**, including email addresses, account IDs and other account information.

Playrion was still part of the Paradox Interactive group when the incident occurred. Paradox had acquired the Paris-based studio in 2020. On the same day as the hack, Paradox announced an agreement to sell Playrion to Trophy Games for €2.25 million, but the transaction was not scheduled to close until **30 September 2026**.

The incident therefore represented the most direct cyber escalation of the Turko–Paradox War: a group identifying itself with the Turkish side had moved from online protest, mass reviews and support campaigns to compromising the support system of a company still owned by Paradox.

---

## Valve intervenes

Steam later classified part of the sudden negative-review wave as **“off-topic review activity.”**

The reviews themselves were not necessarily deleted, but Steam excluded the affected period from the default calculation of the game's Review Score. This significantly reduced the visible impact of the Turkish review campaign on *Hearts of Iron IV*'s long-term rating.

Valve had therefore become a third major institutional actor in the conflict, intervening to prevent a political or community dispute from permanently determining a game's aggregate Steam rating.

---

## International support

The Turkish campaign also received support from outside Türkiye.

The most unusual example came from the developer of *[Neolithic]To the End*. In the game's 25 September developer diary, the developer titled an entire section **“The Turko-Paradox war”**, wrote **“I stand with Turkey on this!”**, and added two versions of a Mustafa Kemal Atatürk portrait as in-game furniture. Vendors that sold the Turkish flag were also updated to sell the portraits. The protest had therefore crossed into the content of an unrelated video game.

The incident had spread far beyond the original Discord server and had begun influencing the content and public communications of unrelated game developers.

---

## Aftermath

Within only a few days, a ban appeal on a Discord server had developed into one of the largest community crises surrounding a Paradox title.

The Turkish gaming community succeeded in forcing the controversy to the level of Paradox's senior management, generated tens of thousands of negative Steam reviews, brought prominent streamers and historians into the campaign, produced more than **300 bans of Turkish users** on the official Discord, temporarily forced the server to close itself to new members, and compelled Paradox to acknowledge inconsistent moderation and rewrite its Discord rules.

Paradox itself subsequently acknowledged that support tickets and messages submitted during the backlash had been used to help shape the changes it introduced.

The confrontation also crossed into cybersecurity when a Paradox-owned studio's support email system was compromised and used to distribute a message titled **“HACKED BY TURKS.”**

However, the fundamental disagreement remained unresolved as of 25 September.

Paradox accepted responsibility for the way the incident had been handled, but **did not issue the explicit apology demanded by the Turkish community, did not publicly retract the accusation linking Atatürk to the Armenian Genocide, and did not announce the removal of the moderator at the center of the controversy.**

For that reason, many Turkish participants regarded the rule change not as the conclusion of the Turko–Paradox War, but as only a partial retreat by Paradox.
